Glossary

Autonomous mobile robot AMR

A driverless vehicle that carries materials and chooses its own path, avoiding obstacles. It differs from an AGV, which follows a fixed route. It is not a cobot, but it shares aisles with people and can carry a collaborative arm.

Cobot (collaborative robot)

A robot designed to be used near people, without a fence around it, with safety functions such as force and speed limiting. The robot alone does not make the workstation safe: what is or is not collaborative is the whole application, with gripper, part and task.

Related standards: ISO 10218-1:2025 · ISO 10218-2:2025

Collaborative application

The combination of robot, gripper or tool, part, workstation and task in which person and robot share the same space during production. It is the application that goes through risk assessment, not the robot on its own.

Related standards: ISO 10218-2:2025 · ISO/TS 15066:2016

Digital twin

A virtual model of a piece of equipment, cell or process that receives data from the real system and lets you simulate and test changes before applying them. Without the link to real data, it is only a simulation.

End-effector (gripper or tool)

What is attached to the end of the robot arm to perform the task: gripper, suction cup, screwdriver, welding torch. Together with the part, it is often the most hazardous element of a collaborative application.

Related standards: ISO/TR 20218-1:2018

Ergonomics

The study of how to fit work to people. Physical ergonomics deals with postures, forces and repetition; cognitive ergonomics deals with attention, decision-making and mental workload.

Human-robot collaboration HRC

Work in which person and robot act in the same space toward a common goal. Research usually distinguishes levels: coexistence (side by side, no shared task), cooperation (same task, at different times) and collaboration (same part, at the same time).

Industrial IoT IIoT

Sensors, machines and systems networked on the factory floor, collecting and exchanging data about what happens in production. It is what feeds the digital twin and data-based maintenance.

Industry 5.0

A vision proposed by the European Commission that complements Industry 4.0 with three pillars: human-centricity, sustainability and resilience. Technology serves the person, not the other way around.

Payload

The maximum mass the robot can move at the end of its arm. It includes the weight of the gripper or tool, not only the part.

Performance level PL

A measure of how reliable a safety function of the control system is, in five levels from "a" to "e". The risk assessment determines the level required of each function.

Related standards: ISO 13849-1:2023

Power and force limiting PFL

A mode of operation in which contact between person and robot is allowed, provided force and pressure stay below limits defined for each body region. It requires measuring or calculating those forces in the real application.

Related standards: ISO/TS 15066:2016 · ISO/PAS 5672:2023

Programming by demonstration

A way of programming in which the person shows the task to the robot, by moving the arm or performing the motion, instead of writing code. It reduces the need for a programming specialist.

Psychosocial risk

A health risk linked to how work is organized and to relationships at work: imposed pace, little autonomy, monitoring, job insecurity. The arrival of a robot can affect several of these factors.

Related standards: ISO 45003:2021 · NR-01 (Brasil)

Risk assessment

The process of identifying the hazards of a machine or application, estimating and evaluating the risks, and deciding on measures to reduce them. It is required for every cobot application and must be redone when the task, gripper or part changes.

Related standards: ISO 12100:2010 · ISO 10218-2:2025

Safety integrity level SIL

Another scale for the reliability of safety functions, used by IEC 62061. It is the alternative route to the performance level (PL).

Related standards: IEC 62061:2021

Small and medium-sized enterprises PME · SME · pyme

Smaller companies, which make up most of industry. They are the audience to which the cobot is usually presented as a solution, and also where adoption meets the most barriers.

Speed and separation monitoring SSM

A mode of operation in which sensors track the distance between person and robot. The robot slows down as the person approaches and stops before there is contact.

Related standards: ISO/TS 15066:2016 · ISO 13855:2024

Trust in the robot

How much the person relies on the robot to do what is expected of it. Too little trust leads to not using the robot; too much leads to dropping one's guard. It is not the same as the robot actually being trustworthy.
