Electronics

Testing, inspection and handling of boards and small products, in varied batches and with care against electrostatic discharge.

Typical applications

  • Loading and unloading boards into test fixtures
  • Optical inspection of boards and assemblies
  • Pick-and-place of larger components and connectors
  • Screwdriving small enclosures
  • Packing and labelling finished products

In electronics, small components are already placed by dedicated high-speed machines. Cobots appear around those machines: in testing, inspection, final assembly and packing, where there is still a lot of manual handling and products change often.

Why cobots here

  • Small batches, many models: a cobot cell can switch products by changing the program and the fixture.
  • Monotonous tasks: loading and unloading boards into test equipment for hours is tiring and error-prone.
  • Limited space: cobots usually fit on existing benches, without fences around them.

Where IoT and the digital twin come in

  • Serial-number traceability: each board carries its test and inspection results.
  • Real-time test data: failure patterns show up quickly and can point to a problem in an earlier process step.
  • Line balancing: the digital twin helps decide how many stations and cobots each product mix needs.

Watch out for

  • Electrostatic discharge (ESD): grippers, suction cups and surfaces touching the boards must be suitable for ESD-protected areas.
  • Precision: fitting connectors and components requires repeatability within tolerance; sometimes computer vision or force sensing is needed to guide the insertion.
  • Risk assessment: even with light parts, the tool and the cobot’s motion must be assessed for people working at the bench.
