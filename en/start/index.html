Start here

A six-step path for anyone evaluating their first collaborative robot, written with small and medium-sized companies in mind. Each step points to what is already on the site.

  1. 1

    Understand what a cobot is (and is not)

    A cobot is a robot made to work near people, but on its own it does not make the workstation safe or collaborative: that depends on the task, the gripper and the part. It pays to learn the vocabulary before talking to suppliers.

    • Glossary →
    • Learn: basic concepts →
    • Research: fundamentals and technology →
  2. 2

    Pick the right task

    The first projects that work out are usually repetitive, tiring or awkward-posture tasks, with light parts and little variation. Look at how your sector uses cobots and where research has found limits.

    • Sectors →
    • Research: applications by sector →
  3. 3

    Do the whole calculation

    The robot is only part of the cost: add gripper, integration, safeguards, training and the downtime for installation. Estimate the return with your own numbers and see what studies say about why so many small companies hesitate.

    • ROI calculator →
    • Research: adoption, economics and ethics →
    • Technical catalog →
  4. 4

    Treat safety as part of the project

    Every cobot application needs a risk assessment, redone whenever the task, gripper or part changes. Get to know the standards that apply before you buy, not after you install.

    • Standards →
    • Research: safety and standards →
  5. 5

    Involve people from the start

    Whoever will work next to the robot knows where the task hurts. Pace, autonomy, mental workload and trust change when a cobot arrives, and studies show that involving the operator early matters for acceptance.

    • Research: human factors and ergonomics →
    • Research: Industry 5.0 and work →
    • SDGs: decent work →
  6. 6

    Learn and train the team

    There is free training from manufacturers and a few independent courses. Start with the open material, and leave paid training for when the equipment has been chosen.

    • Courses →
    • Tutorials →
    • Research: cobots in the classroom →

This path is general guidance, written with AI assistance and still under review by the team. It does not replace a risk assessment by a qualified professional.
