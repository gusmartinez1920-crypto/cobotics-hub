Real collaboration demands more from a cobot than avoiding collisions

Editorial for a special issue on intelligent cobots: the editors argue that collaborating requires understanding situations and communicating, and introduce the issue's 10 papers.

Summary by Claude (Anthropic) · Published November 13, 2026

Also available in: Português · Español

Written with AI assistance. Still under review by the team.

Original paper

Intelligent cobot systems: human-cobot collaboration in manufacturing

Maurizio Faccio, Yuval Cohen

Journal of Intelligent Manufacturing, 2023

DOI 10.1007/s10845-023-02142-z

Open access

Why it matters

The text works as a quick map of research topics in intelligent cobots: safety, programming with augmented reality, task allocation, motivation, faults and inspection. It is useful for locating subjects and deciding what to read next, more than as evidence.

Method

This is a three-page editorial with no research of its own. The editors open with a short conceptual introduction and then summarize, in one paragraph each, the 10 papers accepted for the special issue, linked by theme.

Key findings

  • The editors argue that collaborative applications are more complex than those of traditional industrial robots. To truly collaborate, the cobot has to track and understand situations and communicate in a way closer to communication between people.
  • According to the editorial, much of the cobot literature deals with collision avoidance, manipulation movements, precision and safety measures. Understanding human behavior and perception adds a new level of complexity.
  • One of the papers introduced studied 16 participants in a joint sawing task with a cobot that switched among three control strategies. People abandoned the higher-effort modes and preferred the collaborative mode.
  • Another paper reports that digital assistance in assembly improves some motivational characteristics of the work but misses the main goal of relieving cognitive load. A third notes that augmented reality helps novices program but often causes cognitive overload.
  • The issue also brings together work on safe distance with prediction of the human trajectory, task allocation between person and cobot, battery disassembly planned with a digital twin, fault diagnosis and visual inspection.

Limitations

This is a secondary source: the results are summaries of other papers, almost always without numbers, and the editorial does not say how the papers were selected. To use any result, one has to go to the original paper in the issue.
