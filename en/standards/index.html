Standards

The technical standards and regulations that come up most often in collaborative robot projects, each with a short summary and a link to the official source.

This list is a guide. It does not replace reading the standards or the judgment of a qualified professional. Standards change: always check the current edition at the official source. Written with AI assistance and still under review by the team.

Robots and collaborative applications

  • ISO 10218-1:2025

    Robotics — Safety requirements — Part 1: Industrial robots

    Safety requirements for the industrial robot itself, as the manufacturer delivers it: safe design, protective measures and information for use. Integrating the robot into an application is left to Part 2.

    2025 edition; replaces the 2011 edition.

  • ISO 10218-2:2025

    Robotics — Safety requirements — Part 2: Industrial robot applications and robot cells

    Safety of the robot application and the robot cell: integration, commissioning, operation, maintenance and decommissioning. It is the central standard for anyone setting up a cobot workstation, because what is or is not collaborative is the application, not the robot.

    2025 edition; replaces the 2011 edition. According to industry sources, it now carries requirements for collaborative applications that used to be in ISO/TS 15066.

  • ISO/TS 15066:2016

    Robots and robotic devices — Collaborative robots

    Technical specification that supplements ISO 10218 for collaborative systems. It is known for the force and pressure limits for contact between person and robot and for the calculation of the separation distance.

    Still current in the ISO catalogue, but under revision: it will be replaced by a new document in preparation (ISO 15066-1, biomechanical thresholds).

  • ISO/PAS 5672:2023

    Robotics — Collaborative applications — Test methods for measuring forces and pressures in human-robot contacts

    Describes how to measure and analyze forces and pressures in physical contact between person and robot, and what is required of the measuring device. It is used to validate a force-limited application in practice.

    Official source →

  • ISO/TR 20218-1:2018

    Robotics — Safety design for industrial robot systems — Part 1: End-effectors

    Safety guidance for designing and integrating grippers and tools at the end of the robot arm. It matters because the gripper and the part are often the most hazardous element of a collaborative application.

    Technical report (guidance, not requirements); it still refers to the 2011 edition of ISO 10218-2.

  • ISO/TR 20218-2:2017

    Robotics — Safety design for industrial robot systems — Part 2: Manual load/unload stations

    Guidance for stations where a person loads and unloads parts by hand in a robot system: how to restrict access to the hazard zone and keep the workstation ergonomic.

    Technical report (guidance, not requirements); it still refers to the 2011 edition of ISO 10218-2.

  • ISO 8373:2021

    Robotics — Vocabulary

    Defines the terms used in robotics. It is the reference for using words with the same meaning in projects, contracts and technical texts.

    Under revision at ISO.

Machinery safety (foundation)

  • ISO 12100:2010

    Safety of machinery — General principles for design — Risk assessment and risk reduction

    The foundation of machinery safety: how to identify hazards, estimate and evaluate risks, and reduce them over the life of the machine. The risk assessment of a cobot cell follows this method.

    Under revision at ISO.

    Official source →

  • ISO 13849-1:2023

    Safety of machinery — Safety-related parts of control systems — Part 1: General principles for design

    Method and requirements for designing the parts of the control system that perform safety functions, including software. It is the source of the "performance level" (PL) required of the robot's safety functions.

    Official source →

  • IEC 62061:2021

    Safety of machinery — Functional safety of safety-related control systems

    Requirements for designing, integrating and validating safety-related control systems for machines. It is the alternative route to ISO 13849-1, using the safety integrity level (SIL).

    With Amendment 1, from 2024.

    Official source →

  • ISO 13855:2024

    Safety of machinery — Positioning of safeguards with respect to the approach of the human body

    How to position and size safeguards (light curtains, scanners, pressure-sensitive mats, two-hand controls) with respect to a person approaching. It is used to calculate distances when the robot slows or stops on detecting someone.

    2024 edition; replaces the 2010 edition, which had a different title.

    Official source →

  • ISO 13850:2015

    Safety of machinery — Emergency stop function — Principles for design

    Requirements and design principles for the emergency stop function on machinery, whatever the type of energy.

    Under revision at ISO.

    Official source →

People: ergonomics and psychosocial risks

  • ISO 11228-1:2021

    Ergonomics — Manual handling — Part 1: Lifting, lowering and carrying

    Recommended limits for lifting, lowering and carrying objects of 3 kg or more by hand, according to intensity, frequency and duration. It helps decide which tasks are worth handing over to the cobot.

    Official source →

  • ISO 45003:2021

    Occupational health and safety management — Psychological health and safety at work — Guidelines for managing psychosocial risks

    Guidelines for managing psychosocial risks within an occupational health and safety management system (ISO 45001). It does not mention robots, but it covers what the arrival of a cobot can affect: pace, autonomy, mental workload and monitoring.

    It is guidance, not a certifiable standard.

    Official source →

Mobile robots

  • ISO 3691-4:2023

    Industrial trucks — Safety requirements and verification — Part 4: Driverless industrial trucks and their systems

    Safety requirements and verification for driverless industrial trucks (AGVs and autonomous mobile robots) and their systems. It applies when the cobot is mounted on a mobile base or shares the aisle with these vehicles.

    Under revision at ISO.

    Official source →

Laws and regulations

  • NR-12 (Brasil)

    Segurança no Trabalho em Máquinas e Equipamentos

    Brazilian regulatory standard, legally binding, on machinery safety. Item 12.1.12 treats robotic systems that follow ABNT ISO 10218-1, ABNT ISO 10218-2 and ISO/TS 15066 as compliant.

    The text says "robotic systems" (it does not use the word "collaborative") and cites the standards without a year.

    Official source →

  • NR-01 (Brasil)

    Disposições Gerais e Gerenciamento de Riscos Ocupacionais

    Brazilian regulatory standard that organizes how companies manage occupational risks. Chapter 1.5 now expressly includes work-related psychosocial risk factors.

    New chapter 1.5 in force since May 26, 2026 (Ordinance MTE 1.419/2024, postponed by Ordinance 765/2025).

    Official source →

  • Regulamento (UE) 2023/1230

    Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 on machinery

    The new European machinery regulation: it sets the health and safety requirements for machinery placed on the European Union market and replaces Directive 2006/42/EC. It matters to anyone exporting machines or cells to Europe.

    Mandatory from January 20, 2027; until then the Directive applies.

    Official source →

  • Regulamento (UE) 2024/1689 — AI Act

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — Artificial Intelligence Act

    The European regulation on artificial intelligence, applied in stages. An AI system can be classified as high-risk when it is a safety component of a regulated product, such as vision that detects a person and stops the robot.

    The timeline was changed in 2026: according to the European Commission, the high-risk rules for AI embedded in regulated products apply from August 2, 2028. Check the current timeline at the source.

    Official source →
