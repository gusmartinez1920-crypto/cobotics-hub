Cobots by sector: where they already work and what the research shows

From welding to the lab, 16 studies show where cobots already appear and where the evidence is thin. In warehouses, construction, and farming, the robot studied is rarely a collaborative arm.

By Claude (Anthropic) · Published November 13, 2026

Also available in: Español · Português

Written with AI assistance. Still under review by the team.

Cobots already appear from welding to the laboratory, but the evidence is uneven. In the 16 summaries this synthesis draws on, almost nothing was measured with a collaborative arm in production: concepts, simulations, reviews, and lab tests dominate. In several sectors the robot studied is not even an arm, but a vehicle, a construction machine, or a service robot.

In the factory, the question is who does each task

Neither of the two manufacturing studies measured a cell in operation. In a welding cell concept, the welder takes the short, complex seams and the robot takes the long, straight ones; the robot welds only when the operator is outside the area (Müller et al., 2017). In an optimization model for assembly lines, tested on 164 configurations, including ergonomics cut the risk indicators by about a quarter. Adding robots showed diminishing returns: in one line, the fourth robot brought no gain (Kulaç & Kiraz, 2026).

In the warehouse, the robot is a vehicle and acceptance matters

A review of 36 warehouse studies found mostly guided vehicles and mobile robots. Productivity gains in order picking depend on planning and a good division of tasks (Jacob et al., 2023). In an experiment with 60 students in a laboratory warehouse, picking orders with a mobile robot raised satisfaction compared with manual picking (Pasparakis et al., 2023). With an autonomous order-picking truck, planning the route together with the stretches on foot cut travel time by 17.4% on average, on real pick lists from a distribution center (Tutam & De Koster, 2024).

On the construction site, factory rules are not enough

A review of 287 studies concluded that the ISO 10218 standard is insufficient for the job site; in that review, the collaborative arm is a minority next to teleoperated machines and exoskeletons (Lin et al., 2025). A taxonomy of 16 categories provides vocabulary to describe human roles, autonomy, and safety (Rodrigues et al., 2023). Another paper, in which cobot means construction machines, warns that automating an isolated task can cut the informal exchange of information on site (Schöberl et al., 2023). In the only construction test with a collaborative arm, ten participants with an architecture background previewed a cobot’s motion in augmented reality, and the cobot still collided several times with the structure (Loy et al., 2025). In virtual reality, with 30 people, separating the work areas of a person and a masonry robot increased perceived safety (You et al., 2018). A proposal to generate instructions for people and robots with artificial intelligence did not reach execution (Das et al., 2026).

In the field and the meat plant, the product is never the same

A review of 32 studies on mobile agricultural machines found that the human-robot partnership improved fruit detection in every case compared, but most of the evidence comes from simulation (Benos et al., 2023). On a farm, wearable sensors on 20 participants recognized six steps of handing a crate to a stationary agricultural vehicle, with a mean accuracy of 85.6% (Anagnostis et al., 2021). In meat cutting, a narrative review shows that the cobot’s force limiting does not protect when the tool is a knife; the cell it proposes was not built (Romanov et al., 2022).

In healthcare, the map is wide and the test is small

A narrative review of 47 studies describes mostly service, surgical, and rehabilitation robots, and names cost, privacy, and liability for harm as open issues (Weerarathna et al., 2023). In a microbiology laboratory, a cobot taught by demonstration picked 56 bacterial colonies, and about 11% of the samples were not identified. Deposition taught by hand was replaced by a programmed trajectory (Baumkircher et al., 2022).

What is still unknown

The studies with people use small samples or students, not workers from the sector (Pasparakis et al., 2023; You et al., 2018; Anagnostis et al., 2021). Several gains are stated without measurement, such as efficiency in welding and the speed-up in earthworks (Müller et al., 2017; Schöberl et al., 2023). The reviews point to a lack of field studies in warehouses and of validation on real construction sites (Jacob et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2025). Several studies include no risk assessment (Baumkircher et al., 2022; Loy et al., 2025).

In practice

  • Check which robot the study used: in warehouses, construction, and farming, the term cobot often covers vehicles and machines, not arms (Jacob et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2025; Benos et al., 2023).
  • In a line, start with repetitive or high-risk tasks; more robots do not always bring more gain (Kulaç & Kiraz, 2026).
  • With a dangerous tool, do not rely only on the cobot’s force limiting: it does not protect against the knife (Romanov et al., 2022).
