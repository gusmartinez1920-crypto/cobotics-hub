There is a cost, but the price depends on how the cell is set up. Robot speed, predictable motion, and who controls the pace change the stress and mental workload (the effort of attention a task demands) of the person working beside the machine. This synthesis draws on 14 summaries, mostly lab experiments and reviews.

## How the robot moves changes how the person feels

A review of 114 studies found that, with industrial manipulators, people feel safer with greater distance, lower speed, predictable motion, and warning of the motion ([Rubagotti et al., 2022](/en/research/perceived-safety-physical-human-robot-interaction-survey/)). A review of 23 studies on mental workload adds that unpredictable motion and time pressure raise stress ([Carissoli et al., 2023](/en/research/mental-workload-when-working-with-cobots-scoping-review/)).

But slower was not always better. In an experiment with 42 people, high speed worsened perceived safety, while low speed brought more negative emotions ([Gervasi et al., 2022](/en/research/cobot-speed-pace-control-operator-experience/)). In virtual reality, with a mobile robot and 56 adults, trust on the curved path was higher at 1.0 m/s than at 0.5 and 1.5 m/s ([Haney et al., 2026](/en/research/perceived-safety-autonomous-mobile-robot-speed-path/)).

## Whoever controls the pace feels less pressure

When the cobot moved on a fixed timer, without the operator's command, participants reported more pressure and anxiety ([Gervasi et al., 2022](/en/research/cobot-speed-pace-control-operator-experience/)). In an experiment with 17 students, perceived workload in sequential tasks averaged 19.06 with the person as leader and 28.82 as follower; the follower also did more parts of the task alone, which may explain part of the difference ([Segura et al., 2025](/en/research/leader-or-follower-role-cobot-cognitive-workload/)). There is a cost: following the robot's pace is more stressful, but productivity is higher when the robot leads ([Carissoli et al., 2023](/en/research/mental-workload-when-working-with-cobots-scoping-review/)).

## Does the cobot relieve the mind or overload it? Studies disagree

In an experiment with 10 students, mental workload measured through brain signals fell with cobot support; the fixed order of the scenarios may have favored the later ones ([Caiazzo et al., 2025](/en/research/mental-workload-and-productivity-in-cobot-assembly-eeg/)). By contrast, in a review of 163 studies on construction, more automation with a robotic arm was associated with more fatigue, stress, and cognitive load than manual execution ([Antwi et al., 2026](/en/research/human-factors-human-robot-collaboration-in-construction/)). Familiarity matters: in a test with 14 people, reported workload was lower with a cobot that displayed its current action, and inexperienced participants tended to find automation more demanding ([Lagomarsino et al., 2023](/en/research/cognitive-load-cobot-assembly-measured-by-camera/)).

## For the body, always helping the same way has a downside

Adapting the cobot to posture is the most mature area; for cognitive load, there is no accepted method or set of metrics ([Lorenzini et al., 2023](/en/research/physical-cognitive-ergonomics-human-robot-collaboration-review/)). A planner that hands the most fatiguing steps to the cobot cut perceived exertion from 4.56 to 2.31 in a test with 8 people ([You & Ji, 2025](/en/research/reinforcement-learning-cobot-takes-most-tiring-tasks/)). But handing over the part at the same spot every time repeats the same movement. In an experiment with 16 people, varying the handover point cut the time in high-risk posture from 13.86% to 8.87% of handovers ([Zolotas et al., 2024](/en/research/cobot-that-varies-handovers-to-avoid-repetitive-motion/)).

## Too much trust also has a cost, and trust is poorly measured

Robot failures reduce trust, and excessive trust was associated with more unsafe behavior ([Antwi et al., 2026](/en/research/human-factors-human-robot-collaboration-in-construction/)). In a household task, a robot that estimates the person's trust improved team performance; the policy that seeks performance outdid the one that seeks maximum trust ([Chen et al., 2020](/en/research/robot-that-considers-human-trust-when-deciding/)). A position paper warns that several well-known scales measure how trustworthy the robot seems, not the person's willingness to be exposed to it ([Onnasch et al., 2026](/en/research/trust-in-robots-is-not-the-same-as-trustworthiness/)).

## What is still unknown

Almost everything comes from the lab, with short tasks and volunteers. In the mental workload review, only 2 of 23 studies took place in a factory ([Carissoli et al., 2023](/en/research/mental-workload-when-working-with-cobots-scoping-review/)). In the construction review, 95% of studies lasted less than an 8-hour shift ([Antwi et al., 2026](/en/research/human-factors-human-robot-collaboration-in-construction/)). Only three studies on perceived safety lasted up to two months ([Rubagotti et al., 2022](/en/research/perceived-safety-physical-human-robot-interaction-survey/)). And the proposal of proactive collaboration, in which the robot anticipates the person, offers no indicator to measure operator well-being ([Li et al., 2021](/en/research/proactive-human-robot-collaboration-conceptual-proposal/)).

## In practice

- Give the operator control of the pace and, where possible, the lead in the task ([Gervasi et al., 2022](/en/research/cobot-speed-pace-control-operator-experience/); [Segura et al., 2025](/en/research/leader-or-follower-role-cobot-cognitive-workload/)).
- Prefer predictable motion with warning, and test the speed: neither the highest nor the lowest speed was rated best ([Rubagotti et al., 2022](/en/research/perceived-safety-physical-human-robot-interaction-survey/); [Haney et al., 2026](/en/research/perceived-safety-autonomous-mobile-robot-speed-path/)).
- Show the operator what the robot is doing, especially those with no robotics experience ([Lagomarsino et al., 2023](/en/research/cognitive-load-cobot-assembly-measured-by-camera/)).
- Assess ergonomics beyond a single cycle: the same handover point, repeated every time, can force repetitive movements ([Zolotas et al., 2024](/en/research/cobot-that-varies-handovers-to-avoid-repetitive-motion/)).
