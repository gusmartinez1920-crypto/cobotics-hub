In food and beverage, much of the manual work happens **after** the product is packaged: erecting cases, placing packs inside them, stacking pallets and checking labels. That is where most collaborative cells tend to be installed.

## Why cobots here

- **End of line:** case packing and palletizing are repetitive and physically heavy.
- **Format changes:** the same line packs different products and sizes during the week; a cobot can switch palletizing patterns by program.
- **Harsh environments:** cold rooms and wet areas are uncomfortable for long shifts.

## Where IoT and the digital twin come in

- **Temperature and humidity:** sensors track the cold chain and storage conditions.
- **Quality:** cameras and sensors check label, expiry date, seal and fill level.
- **Packaging-line simulation:** the digital twin helps predict bottlenecks and the effect of each format change.

## Watch out for

- **Hygiene:** where there is **direct contact with food**, the robot, gripper and lubricants must be food-grade and the equipment must withstand the area's cleaning process (including washdown, where applicable). Many applications avoid direct contact and handle only packaged product.
- **Weight and pace:** full pallets and heavy cases can exceed a cobot's payload, and collaborative speeds may not keep up with very fast lines.
- **Risk assessment:** collaborative palletizers share space with operators and forklifts; the whole area must be assessed.
