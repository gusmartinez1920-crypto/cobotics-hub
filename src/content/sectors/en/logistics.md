In warehouses and distribution centers, work is dominated by **moving things**: taking cases off pallets, picking order items, building kits and carrying materials from one point to another. Cobots appear both at fixed stations and mounted on **autonomous mobile robots (AMRs)**, forming so-called mobile manipulators.

## Why cobots here

- **Repetitive lifting:** palletizing and depalletizing mean lifting cases all day.
- **Variable demand:** seasonal peaks call for automation that can be relocated without construction work.
- **Shared space:** people, carts and robots move through the same aisles.

## Where IoT and the digital twin come in

- **Tracking:** barcode and RFID readers and sensors at the stations show where every order is in real time.
- **Warehouse simulation:** the digital twin lets you test layouts, mobile-robot routes and the effect of an order peak before changing the real world.
- **Robot fleet:** battery, route and stop data help coordinate mobile robots and people.

## Watch out for

- **Payload and item variety:** heavy cases or very different items (shapes, fragile packaging) need suitable grippers and vision — and may exceed what a cobot can pick.
- **Pace:** collaborative speed must be compared with the orders-per-hour target.
- **Mobile robots:** an arm moving on a base that also moves adds risks; the risk assessment must cover the arm, the base and the aisles they travel.
