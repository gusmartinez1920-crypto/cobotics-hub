whyItMatters: "It is a good entry point for newcomers: it defines human-robot collaboration, separates the four types of interaction between person and robot, and shows which themes the research concentrates on. It helps place a project or a reading within the field."
method: "The authors searched the Scopus database for the terms human-robot collaboration and human-robot interaction, with no time limit, and selected the 2,000 most-cited papers. They analyzed the network formed by the keywords of those papers. They then wrote a narrative review of the trends, with examples of case studies."
findings:
  - "The keyword network has four groups: artificial intelligence, learning and human intention (the densest); assembly, digital twin, and virtual and augmented reality; safety and risks; ergonomics and trust between person and machine."
  - "The review adopts four types of interaction: coexistence (same environment, no interaction), synchronization (same environment, at different times), cooperation (at the same time, on different tasks) and collaboration (a joint task in which one's action immediately affects the other)."
  - "In safety, sensors fall into two types, contact and contactless, and the approaches into two: before and after the collision."
  - "In ergonomics, the review reports that it is up to the robot to adapt its behavior so that the person works safely and comfortably, and that assessment uses simulation with digital human models."
  - "Assembly and welding are named as the two main industrial applications. Outside industry, the review cites surgery, rehabilitation, agriculture and education."
limitations: "The paper reports no review protocol or inclusion criteria, and selecting by citation count favors older, better-known work. Part of the text deals with surgical, agricultural and social robots, not industrial cobots. Adoption barriers, cost, ethics and regulation are not discussed."
