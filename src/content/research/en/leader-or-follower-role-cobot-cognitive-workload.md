whyItMatters: "When designing a collaborative cell, people tend to think about physical ergonomics and forget who decides the pace and order of actions. This study treats the operator's role, leader or follower, as a design variable and shows that it affects mental workload. It is an inexpensive decision to make early in the design."
method: "The work has two parts. In the first, the authors analyzed 50 collaborative applications from the literature using a framework with four components: interaction level, work role, communication interface and safety controls. In the second, 17 undergraduate students, trained for a semester in collaborative robotics, inspected the paint on wooden blocks with a UR3 cobot in four scenarios that combined supportive or sequential interaction with the leader or follower role. They measured workload (NASA-TLX), spare mental capacity (Bedford Scale) and average heart rate."
findings:
  - "In the survey of 50 applications, the person appears as follower only in manual part configuration, the most frequent task. In assemblies with large loads and in precise positioning, the person is mostly the leader."
  - "The scenario had a significant effect on overall NASA-TLX workload (p = 0.019). In sequential interaction, the mean was 19.06 with the person as leader and 28.82 with the person as follower."
  - "On the Bedford Scale, where a lower score is better, the scenarios with the person as leader indicated more spare mental capacity (means of 2.71 and 2.59) than those with the person as follower (3.65 and 3.47), whatever the interaction level."
  - "Average heart rate did not differ across scenarios (76.88 to 78.12 beats per minute), although it had a moderate positive correlation with overall workload (r = 0.39)."
  - "Sequential interactions were more sensitive to the change of role; supportive ones barely changed. Even as followers, participants rated the workload as satisfactory, and the authors suggest this role for training novices."
limitations: "The sample is 17 students trained by the authors themselves, in a laboratory, with a light task and scenarios lasting 3.5 to 5.5 minutes; there are no industry operators, full shifts or production pressure. In the follower role, the person also carried out more parts of the task alone, which may explain part of the difference attributed to the role. Only the subjective measures distinguished the scenarios."
