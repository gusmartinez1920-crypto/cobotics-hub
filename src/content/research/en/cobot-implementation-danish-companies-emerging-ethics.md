whyItMatters: "People planning to buy a cobot usually look at the return on investment and the promise of collaboration. This study shows what happens after the purchase, on the shop floor: the effort to get the cobot working, the reliance on motivated individuals and the effect on those who lose part of their task. It is a warning for anyone preparing an implementation."
method: "A qualitative, ethnographically inspired study with semi-structured interviews at 15 Danish companies between June and December 2019: 8 with fewer than 250 employees, 3 with 250 to 500 and 4 with more than 500. Each interview took place at the company, after a presentation of the process and observation of the facilities, using a 24-question guide. The companies had 28 cobots in total, almost all from Universal Robots. The author coded the answers and arrived at four themes: learning, values, human skill and the reconfiguration of work."
findings:
  - "In no company was there direct interaction between a person and the cobot during the operating cycle, only between cycles or after them. In practice, the cobot is programmed to do as much of the task as possible and is seen as a cheap industrial robot, not as a teammate."
  - "The return-on-investment calculation becomes ambiguous because jobs are modified rather than eliminated: removing parts of a job is not the same as saving labor."
  - "Getting the cobot to work takes long cycles of trial and error, in projects that run alongside production and range from an expected 2 or 3 months to several years. Success depends on a highly motivated person who learns on their own."
  - "Safety criteria vary: some companies measured the forces in every imaginable contact, while others accepted the cobot by comparing it to the everyday bumps of factory life. When more speed is needed, the unplanned cost of barriers and the loss of floor space appear."
  - "The operator's skill only becomes visible when someone tries to copy it. On one press brake, the project stopped after 6 months because the operator gave the part a slight movement that the cobot could only reproduce with a much slower cycle."
limitations: "The sample is Danish only, and the study does not report how many people were interviewed or provide numerical measures. The results are qualitative and cannot be generalized to other countries or sectors."
