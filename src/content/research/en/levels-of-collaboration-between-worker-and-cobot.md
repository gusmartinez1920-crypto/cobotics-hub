whyItMatters: "Coexistence, cooperation and collaboration mean different things from one author to the next, which gets in the way of designing and evaluating a cobot cell. This study offers a common vocabulary that serves both the safety analysis and the question of what the worker experiences at the station."
method: "The authors reviewed previously published classifications of collaboration levels and proposed their own framework, with levels and sublevels. They then applied it to two cases from the research institute where they work: assembling seals with an ABB YuMi robot, and a robot that holds the part during welding. Technical feasibility, productivity and cycle time were out of scope."
findings:
  - "The framework has four levels: no coexistence (physical separation), coexistence (shared space with no common goal), cooperation (a common goal in a shared space) and collaboration (simultaneous work on the same object). The sublevels come from four factors: space, robot activity while the person is present, type of joint effort and physical contact."
  - "According to the authors, collaboration has no single factor that would let it be ranked on a simple scale; it has to be described by several factors. Describing it only by the four safeguarding modes of ISO/TS 15066 is not enough from the worker's point of view."
  - "Coexistence and cooperation can be the same for safety and different for the person: when the person's task depends on the robot, timing, the clarity of the robot's motion and shared awareness of the situation start to matter."
  - "Separating the main task, which takes up almost all of the time, from the secondary ones (clearing faults, setup, programming) gives a more faithful classification. The safety solution should be chosen for the whole system, not only for the main task."
  - "In both cases, the main task did not reach the collaboration level: the assembly with the YuMi is coexistence and the welding is sequential cooperation, without contact. The version in which the robot follows the welding would be collaboration, but the authors consider it difficult with the safety technology available."
limitations: "The framework is qualitative and was applied to only two cases, both from the authors' own institution. Metrics that combine safety, worker experience and productivity were left for future work."
