method: "The authors adapted the long Danish version into Portuguese in 2013 and collected data from April 2013 to July 2015. The respondents were 745 workers aged 18 to 65, in 34 public and private companies in northern and central Portugal, from several sectors; 394 answered again about two weeks later. The analysis covered the internal consistency of the scales, stability between the two administrations, floor and ceiling effects, and structure through exploratory factor analysis."
findings:
  - "The Portuguese version keeps the content and structure of the original: 128 items, 41 scales and 7 domains, with scales from 0 to 100 and about 30 minutes to complete."
  - "Internal consistency was above the reference value in 29 of the 41 scales and below 0.60 in three: influence at work, variation and predictability. Test-retest stability was acceptable in 33 of the 41 scales."
  - "Compared with the Danish study, Portuguese workers had higher means for stress (43.9 against 26.7), sleeping troubles (38.7 against 21.3) and job insecurity (43.9 against 23.7). The authors attribute the difference to an unstable labor market."
  - "Factor analysis confirmed the expected structure in 27 scales and not in 14. For 11 of these the authors propose an explanation linked to the way the items combine; for meaning of work, stress and depressive symptoms, they call for further studies."
  - "About 22% of participants did not answer the scales on quality of leadership and support from supervisors, mostly because they considered them not applicable."
limitations: "Manufacturing and retail are poorly represented in the sample, and the authors themselves call for studies in these sectors. The answers are the workers' own reports, collected during a period of economic crisis that may have influenced them, and online collection had lower participation."
