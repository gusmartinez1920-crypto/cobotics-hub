whyItMatters: "A robot may never touch anyone and still leave a person feeling unsafe. The study names the factors that make up that feeling and shows ways to measure it, by questionnaire and by body signals. It uses a social robot, not a cobot: applying it to the shop floor is a hypothesis of the authors and of the reader, which the study did not test."
method: "After reviewing the concept across several fields, the authors proposed a definition of perceived safety and tested it in the lab with 27 young adults, most without technical training or previous contact with robots. Participants played a quiz game with a social robot, which in each condition was manipulated to lower comfort, predictability, sense of control or trust. Questionnaires followed each condition; a camera recorded facial expression and a wristband recorded skin activity and heartbeats."
findings:
  - "Perceived safety correlated positively with the three factors measured: most strongly with comfort (0.78), then with sense of control (0.72) and with trust (0.67)."
  - "Invading personal space with insistent speech and failing at speech recognition lowered perceived safety compared with the baseline condition. The recognition failure had the lowest mean. Whether the failure came at the start or the end of the interaction did not change the scores."
  - "Short unpredictable behaviors that did not disturb the robot's main function did not change the scores. The authors warn that this may not hold when unpredictability affects performance."
  - "People higher in neuroticism felt less safe and less in control. Men reported more safety and more control than women (safety: 3.67 versus 3.06 on average across conditions)."
  - "The physiological signals from the wristband classified perceived safety as high or low with accuracy of up to 0.79, against about 0.56 for facial expression. The interviews revealed one more factor: the transparency of the robot's behavior."
limitations: "The study used a social robot in a game, with a small sample of young adults in a lab and the experimenter nearby, which, according to participants, increased the feeling of safety. The authors only assume that the factors hold for robot arms. In the physiological analysis, 11 participants were dropped because of poor signal quality."
