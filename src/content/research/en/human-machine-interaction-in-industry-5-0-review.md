whyItMatters: "The study is not about cobots: it deals with interaction between people and machines in general, with sensors, networks and artificial intelligence, and the link to collaborative robots is left to the reader. Even so, it provides the Industry 5.0 frame in which cobots fit and gathers concepts useful for designing any automated workstation, such as trust, mental workload and the irony of automation."
method: "The authors reviewed the literature in six article databases, with two keyword searches, and kept texts in English related to manufacturing; for technology articles, only those published from 2019 onward. Of the 234 articles considered relevant or less relevant, 152 entered the review. The synthesis is narrative and follows a classification proposed by the authors themselves."
findings:
  - "The authors organize the topic into four parts, following the path of the signal in the factory: sensors, data processing, transmission and, finally, interaction and collaboration."
  - "Most factory sensors collect data from the environment and the process, not from people. According to the review, this is because Industry 4.0 is driven by efficiency and because collecting data from workers can violate their privacy."
  - "Extended reality does not always reduce workload: it helps little in simple tasks, headsets are still heavy for long use, and the way information is presented can get in the way."
  - "Automation reduces physical load but generates information to process and can increase mental workload. Under the so-called irony of automation, when the machine takes over part of the task, the operator pays less attention to the rest; the review reports that supervising automated processes is not where people excel."
  - "Among the challenges, the authors stress that human-centricity runs up against companies' pursuit of profit, that small and medium-sized enterprises have neither the funds nor the motivation to invest, and that deployment needs support from government and policy. They also argue that the factory should ensure psychological safety and privacy in addition to physical safety."
limitations: "The review does not assess the quality of the included articles and does not explain some selection criteria well, which limits its use as a systematic map. Robots appear only in occasional examples, so the text brings no specific evidence on cobots. The authors name the conflict between monitoring workers and protecting their privacy but do not propose how to govern it."
