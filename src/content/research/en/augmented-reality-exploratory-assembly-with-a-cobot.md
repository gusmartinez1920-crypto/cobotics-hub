whyItMatters: "Programming a robot first and running it only afterward leaves little room to change your mind in the middle of assembly. The study shows how augmented reality can make the cobot's motion and limits visible to people who do not know robotics. It also shows the limits of the idea: too much information in the field of view and collisions that the preview did not prevent."
method: "The authors built a system that links augmented reality glasses (HoloLens 2) to a UR10 cobot. Interaction is turn-based: the person sees the planned path, evaluates it and only then tells the robot to execute. They evaluated the system in two consecutive studies, each with 5 participants trained in architecture: a 2-hour workshop in Australia and a 3-day workshop in Singapore, assembling a lightweight structure of wooden dowels. They collected questionnaires, statements, videos and a 3D scan of the structure and carried out a thematic analysis."
findings:
  - "Usability was rated 7 out of 10 in the first study and 8.8 out of 10 in the second. All participants found it useful to see the cobot's motion before execution, especially for people with no robotics background."
  - "In the first study, control through a visual marker failed with lighting, with misalignments and noticeable lag, which reduced trust. At the participants' request, the second study replaced the marker with a virtual control."
  - "Four out of five participants said that overlaid arrows and colors helped them understand the cobot's limits. The narrow field of view of the glasses, however, made the information overwhelming, and they asked for an interface that shows only the essentials."
  - "Even with the preview, the cobot had several unexpected collisions with the structure already assembled. The 3D scan also showed that the structure deformed during assembly."
  - "The authors report that participants shifted from passive operators to active supervisors, and that reliance on visual cues decreased as assembly progressed."
limitations: "The sample is small (10 people) and the evaluation is qualitative: cognitive load and the effectiveness of augmented reality were not measured. The setting is academic workshops with designers, not a construction site or construction workers. The study does not address risk assessment or safety standards, and collision detection was left to the user."
