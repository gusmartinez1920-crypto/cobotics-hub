The link to cobots is the reader's: it provides the definitions of psychosocial risk and emerging risk and describes work intensification, with machines that set the pace. These are useful questions for anyone installing a cobot in the name of productivity."
method: "The agency consulted experts in three rounds, by e-mail, in 2003 and 2004 (Delphi method). In the first, they proposed the risks; in the following ones, they rated each on a scale of 1 to 5. Responses came from 28, 21 and 16 experts, from 13 European Union countries, the United States and the International Labour Organization, most of them from work psychology. Six literature reviews look more closely at the main risks."
findings:
  - "The report defines an emerging risk as one that is both new and increasing. Psychosocial risks are the aspects of the design, organization and management of work, and of its context, that can cause psychological, social or physical harm."
  - "Of the 42 risks proposed, the experts rated 8 as strongly emerging and 19 as emerging. First on the list are precarious contracts in an unstable labor market, with a mean of 4.56 out of 5."
  - "The top ten fall into five themes: new forms of contract and job insecurity, the aging workforce, work intensification, high emotional demands and poor work-life balance."
  - "On intensification, the report cites 2005 data from 31 countries: 46% of employees worked at very high speed three quarters of the time or more, and machinery set the pace for 40% of workers in manufacturing and mining."
  - "The report notes that intensification leads workers to neutralize machine safety systems to keep up production, and that safety is usually the first thing to give way when demands contradict each other. The link between intensification and accidents was still little researched."
limitations: "The result reflects the opinion of a few experts (16 in the last round), mostly psychologists, who answered a questionnaire in English only. The data are from 2003 and 2004, and technology appears as computerization and machines that set the pace, not as robots. The report works as a conceptual basis, not as evidence on cobots."
