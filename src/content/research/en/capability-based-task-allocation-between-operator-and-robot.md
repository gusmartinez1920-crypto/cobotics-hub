whyItMatters: "In a survey by the authors themselves, deciding where to use collaboration and who does each task emerged as a bigger obstacle than technical problems. The procedure gives a simple path for that decision and makes one priority clear: whatever harms the person's body goes to the robot, regardless of time, cost or quality."
method: "The study has two parts. First, a survey in which 34 respondents, among them 15 robot manufacturers and 14 integrators, rated 17 deployment challenges, plus interviews with 5 user companies. Then the proposed procedure: break the process down into tasks, allocate the tasks with a mandatory decision using an exclusion list of 12 human capabilities and ergonomic limits, and allocate the rest using capability indicators over a catalog of 25 criteria. The proposal was tested qualitatively in two learning factory cases, one in kitting and one in final assembly."
findings:
  - "In the survey, 73% of participants rated as large or very large the challenge of identifying the workstation suited to collaboration and deciding the division of tasks, and 68% said the same of workstation design. Technical challenges, such as reprogramming effort, were not among the top rated."
  - "The authors criticize approaches that generalize the capabilities of \"robots\" and \"humans\": the robot's capability depends on the equipment installed (force sensor, vision) and the person's on their condition. They cite that dexterity falls by about 30% between the ages of 20 and 60."
  - "In the first screening, when criteria conflict, ergonomic risk outweighs capabilities and the task goes to the robot. A task that would cause progressive harm to the person is ruled out for them, regardless of time, cost or quality."
  - "The remaining tasks are compared on three dimensions (process time, additional investment and process quality), with a rating of better, equal or worse for each criterion. The weights, equal at the start, can be adjusted."
  - "In an example with 8 tasks, four were decided by the exclusion list and four by the capability indicators."
limitations: "The proposal was tested only in learning factories, with no measured results for time, quality or ergonomics; the authors themselves say it still has to prove its practicality in industry. The method considers the person in general, not the individual, and ergonomics enters only as physical risk: mental workload and safety are not allocation criteria."
