whyItMatters: "Earlier studies disagree: some say the cobot eases mental workload, others that it increases it, and almost all measure it by questionnaire. This one combines a questionnaire with brain signals captured by electroencephalography (EEG) using a wireless device. It shows a way to assess a workstation that goes beyond the operator's opinion."
method: "Ten engineering students, all men with no experience in assembly or cobots, did the same assembly in three 90-minute scenarios: manual, with a cobot handing over the parts, and with the cobot plus a guide in which a robot prints labels on the part to show the sequence and a camera checks them. The order of the scenarios was fixed, with at least four months between them. Mental workload was estimated with an EEG index and a standardized questionnaire (NASA-TLX); productivity, by the number of correct components out of 75."
findings:
  - "In the manual scenario, mental workload measured by EEG did not change over the 90 minutes. With the cobot it fell over the course of the task; with cobot and guide it was the lowest of the three and also fell."
  - "In the questionnaire, there was a significant difference between scenarios in mental demand, performance, effort and frustration. There was no difference in physical demand or time pressure, which the authors attribute to the light, seated task."
  - "The average number of correct components, out of 75, rose from 47.5 in the manual scenario to 61.3 with the cobot and 72.9 with cobot and guide. The authors report the gain as 18.4% and 15.5%, adding up to 33.9%."
  - "In the open questions, participants said that taking the part from the gripper was safer and more comfortable, that the robot's movement seemed predictable and smooth, and that the bench without a pile of parts was less distracting."
  - "The authors acknowledge that which EEG index is best suited to estimating mental workload in interaction with robots is still under debate."
limitations: "The sample is small and homogeneous: ten young men, all engineering students. The scenarios always came in the same order, and the authors themselves note that the practice gained in the manual scenario may have favored the later ones. The study tests a single task in the lab and does not measure physical load."
