whyItMatters: "The field is an uncertain environment where replacing the worker with an autonomous robot is very difficult; sharing the task between the two emerges as an alternative. The review organizes the modes of joint work and the levels of automation, two classifications that are also useful for people designing cobot applications in the factory. The robots studied are mobile agricultural machines, not industrial collaborative arms."
method: "The authors carried out a systematic review with a protocol defined before the search, in five article databases, with the last search in December 2022. They selected 32 studies, 21 from journals and 11 from conferences, and assessed the quality of each with a risk-of-bias tool. They classified the studies by mode of joint work and by a 10-level automation scale, which runs from the human doing everything to the robot deciding and acting on its own."
findings:
  - "The review describes five modes of joint work: isolation, coexistence, synchronization, cooperation and collaboration. Only the last three involve interaction; collaboration and cooperation were the most frequent in the studies."
  - "The dominant topic is fruit detection. In every case compared, teaming human and robot improved detection, and the result depends heavily on how long the person takes to decide. In the pioneering study, the gain was 4% over manual-only detection and 14% over autonomous-only detection."
  - "In the field, a collaborative site-specific sprayer reduced the pesticide sprayed by 50%."
  - "In strawberry harvesting, 5 robots carrying trays for 25 pickers increased efficiency by 10.2%. In the laboratory, a deposit height of 90 cm on the vehicle avoided large lumbar flexion."
  - "The most studied crops are melon, grape and strawberry, and simulation was the most used method. The weakest point of the studies was sampling: participants were the authors themselves or a few students."
limitations: "Most of the evidence comes from simulation, which does not reproduce the complexity of the field, and the authors themselves call for validation in physical experiments. According to the review, most studies examine how the robot affects people, not how human factors affect the system. Safety is treated in general terms, and the authors acknowledge that the classification of interaction modes comes from industry and is hard to apply in the field."
