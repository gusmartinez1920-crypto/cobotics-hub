whyItMatters: "People who will work beside a robot do not always come from engineering. The study suggests that a person's field of study influences fear, uncertainty and discomfort toward robots, which matters to anyone planning training and the introduction of cobots in mixed teams. The questionnaire can also serve as a model for probing a class's expectations."
method: "An online questionnaire survey, in March and April 2023, with 130 Polish students: 69 from the social sciences and 61 from technical programs. The questionnaire, created by the author, has 20 questions on associations and emotions related to robots, appearance preferences and attitudes toward collaboration at work, with scales from 1 to 7. The comparison between groups used statistical tests at a 5% significance level."
findings:
  - "Technical students reported more interest in new technologies (a mean of 5.90 versus 5.09, on a scale of 1 to 7) and in science fiction (5.08 versus 4.32)."
  - "Social science students mentioned more negative emotions, such as fear and uncertainty; technical students mentioned fewer than half as many and showed more enthusiasm. The differences in enthusiasm, fear and uncertainty were statistically significant."
  - "Discomfort at sharing the workspace or interacting directly with a robot averaged 3.84 in the social science programs and 2.98 in the technical ones."
  - "Both groups prefer robots without a human form for work tasks. The most human-like robot received the worst ratings in every context."
  - "The author attributes the more positive attitude of technical students to contact with robots during their studies, but this exposure was not measured in the study."
limitations: "The study measures stated opinion, with no contact with a real robot, and the robots shown were service, logistics and humanoid robots, not industrial cobots. The author herself acknowledges the small sample, the use of a single questionnaire and the gender imbalance between fields. The reliability of the questionnaire is not reported."
