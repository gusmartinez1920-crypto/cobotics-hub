whyItMatters: "A robot that works well can still fail if people do not accept it. The review organizes what is known about that acceptance in logistics and shows where research is thin, which helps plan deployment with people at the center. Many of the studies deal with mobile warehouse robots rather than collaborative assembly arms."
method: "The authors carried out a systematic literature review in three databases, with the search run in December 2022. From 224 records after removing duplicates they reached 36 studies on warehouses, published between 2017 and 2022, and added 20 studies from other sectors to see what can be transferred. They analyzed the texts with a framework based on a technology acceptance model (UTAUT): performance expectancy, effort expectancy, perceived safety, legal and privacy aspects, and psychosocial factors."
findings:
  - "Laboratory and experimental studies predominate. Most address cobots in general; among specific types, the automated guided vehicle (AGV) is the most frequent, followed by the autonomous mobile robot (AMR)."
  - "Performance: the review found that collaboration in order picking increases productivity, quality and efficiency, but not automatically. It requires planning and a good division of tasks; one of the reviewed studies estimates a gain of up to 10%."
  - "Effort: collaboration relieves repetitive tasks, heavy loads and poor postures, but creates new effort, such as programming and controlling the robot."
  - "Legal and privacy aspects are the least studied factor: they appear in 3 warehouse studies and 2 from other sectors, and only in passing."
  - "On the psychosocial side, the review found lack of information and experience, prejudice against robots and fear of job loss. The authors consider mental factors central to acceptance and note that, at work, collaborating with the robot is rarely a voluntary choice."
limitations: "Field and long-term studies are lacking: the review does not show how acceptance evolves from announcement to daily use, nor the effect of robot failures. Studies disagree about the fear of unemployment, and the review does not measure how many jobs are eliminated or changed. The term cobot is used broadly and mostly covers mobile warehouse robots."
