whyItMatters: "Trust appears in almost every study on cobot acceptance, and this text helps to read those studies more carefully. According to the authors, the scale most used in industrial collaboration measures how trustworthy the robot seems, not the person's willingness to be exposed to what it does. For anyone researching or evaluating a cell, the lesson is to state clearly what is being measured and to include some situation of real risk."
method: "This is a position paper, with no new data. The four authors start from a debate at a scientific meeting on trust in human-robot interaction and review theory and methods in light of a classic model, in which perceived trustworthiness leads to trust and trust, depending on perceived risk, leads to risk-taking behavior. They organize the critique into five confusions, with causes and suggestions, and use their own work as examples of the problems."
findings:
  - "The authors define trust as the willingness to be vulnerable to the robot's actions. It comes before any observable behavior and differs from perceived trustworthiness, which is a judgment about the robot."
  - "According to the authors, several well-known scales, including the one most used in industrial human-robot collaboration, measure perceived trustworthiness rather than trust. One of the authors says she will rename her own scale."
  - "Risk is usually left out of experiments, and without risk trust may not even matter. Next to a caged robot there is no physical risk; beside a cobot there is a risk of collision, and trust may decide whether the person stays alert or stops watching the robot."
  - "Trust is not behavior: it precedes using the robot, relying on it and checking what it does. A review cited by the authors found a weak relationship between stated trust and reliance behavior."
  - "The authors propose stating which element of the model is being measured, using questionnaires that treat trust as an attitude, simulating risk (for example through robot proximity, speed and path) and measuring risk-taking behavior, as well as sharing data and test beds."
limitations: "The text is a reasoned opinion from four researchers, built on a single theoretical model, and it does not test its suggestions. It deals with human-robot interaction in general; the industrial cobot appears only as an example, with no shop-floor data."
