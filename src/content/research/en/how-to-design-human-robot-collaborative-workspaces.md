whyItMatters: "Designing a cobot cell involves more than choosing the robot and avoiding collisions: trust, mental workload, task sharing and work organization all come in. The review works as a map of what is already known about each of these factors and shows where tools are still missing for deciding whether collaboration is feasible for a task."
method: "The authors carried out a systematic literature review in the Scopus and Web of Science databases, from their start to December 2019, in English only, and included 65 papers after screening. They analyzed the content from a human factors and ergonomics perspective and classified the studies into three levels: a single factor (human operator or technology), human-robot team performance, and integrated approaches."
findings:
  - "Of the 65 papers, 16 deal only with the human operator and 19 only with technology; 16 study human-robot team performance and 14 take an integrated approach."
  - "On the operator side, trust is the most studied topic. The review found that early breaches of trust weigh more than late ones and that affective trust better predicts willingness to use the robot."
  - "Avoiding collisions is not enough to ensure worker comfort: safety is understood as the control of physical, psychological and material hazards."
  - "At the team level, the design of the shared space and task allocation dominate, with allocation based on capability, workload, completion time and physical effort."
  - "A person's situational awareness gets worse when the robot alone decides the sequence of tasks, and the review recommends including workers' preferences in the decisions."
limitations: "The search stops in 2019 and did not include terms on sustainability, older workers or people with disabilities, as the authors acknowledge. Most of the studies reviewed did not test complex interaction scenarios or cover all dimensions of human factors, and some of them deal with mobile robots or teleoperation rather than industrial cobots."
