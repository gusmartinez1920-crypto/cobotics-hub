whyItMatters: "Virtual environments are a cheap way to teach collaborative robotics without having a cobot for every student. This study shows that adding real objects to the scene does not improve the experience by itself, and that misalignment between the image and the object can even get in the way. It is a useful warning before investing in mixed reality for training."
method: "An experiment with 12 university students from various fields, 11 of whom returned valid questionnaires. Each one replaced a defective gripper on a virtual UR5 cobot, wearing a virtual reality headset, in two runs: one purely virtual and one with a real assembly workbench aligned with the image, with the order alternating between participants. After each run they answered a standardized 14-item questionnaire on the sense of presence, with open questions about what got in the way."
findings:
  - "There was no statistically significant difference between the two runs in any of the factors measured. General presence and involvement tended to be higher in the purely virtual run; only realism improved slightly with the real workbench."
  - "Participants explained that the task did not require touching the workbench, that contact was indirect, through the controllers, and that the image did not match the real workbench well."
  - "The authors observed that participants moved more cautiously in the run with the real workbench, for fear of hitting an object without knowing what was virtual and what was physical."
  - "Two thirds reported some kind of interference: 25% criticized the blurred image and another 25% the controllers, wishing they could use their own hands."
  - "The height of the calibrated floor varied by about 3 cm at each startup, which made the virtual workbench shift and caused dizziness in some participants."
limitations: "Learning was not measured, only the sense of presence. The sample is small and made up of university students, not apprentices or operators. The technical tracking failures may have hidden the effect, and the greater caution was only observed, not measured. In the task the robot stands still, so there is no collaboration with it in motion."
