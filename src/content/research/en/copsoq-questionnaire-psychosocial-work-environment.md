whyItMatters: "The paper does not cover robots or automation: the link to cobots is the reader's, not the study's. It matters as a measurement tool for anyone who wants to assess workers' demands, stress and well-being, for example before and after a change to a workstation. It also shows the limits of applying a generic questionnaire to very different work settings."
method: "The authors gave a test questionnaire with 165 questions to a representative sample of 1,858 Danish employees (49% women, 62% response rate), by mail or telephone. They built the scales with factor analysis and measures of internal consistency, and examined validity through correlations between scales and a comparison of 32 occupations. The short version was put together by two authors on theoretical grounds, with no statistical procedure."
findings:
  - "The questionnaire has three versions: a long one for research, with 141 questions and 30 dimensions; a medium one for work environment professionals, with 95 questions and 26 dimensions; and a short one for workplaces, with 44 questions and 8 dimensions."
  - "It separates five types of work demands: quantitative, cognitive, emotional, demands for hiding emotions and sensory. It also covers work organization and content, relationships and leadership, insecurity and satisfaction, health and well-being."
  - "The scales run from 0 to 100. In the medium version, the result is compared with the national median and, in groups of 20 or more people, a deviation of 10 points is usually statistically significant."
  - "Data analysis forced changes to most of the scales planned from theory. The repetitive work scale was dropped, and seven scales, almost all of them short, had internal consistency below the reference value of 0.70."
  - "The authors admit gaps: topics such as recognition, career, justice and trust are missing; the quantitative demands scale mixes working hours and work pace; and the leadership questions assume a traditional hierarchy and were often left unanswered."
limitations: "The basis for comparison is the Danish national average; the paper gives no values by industrial sector or for other countries. The authors themselves record the criticism that a generic questionnaire measures very different realities with the same yardstick, and the instrument has no specific scales for work with machines or robots."
