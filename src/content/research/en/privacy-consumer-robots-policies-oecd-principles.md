whyItMatters: "Robots with cameras, microphones and maps of their surroundings collect data on the people around them. The study deals with consumer robots, such as vacuum cleaners and social robots, not industrial cobots: the link to the factory is the reader's. Even so, it offers a guide for reading a data policy and for asking what the robot processes locally and what it sends to the manufacturer."
method: "This is a qualitative analysis of public documents. The authors identified 15 consumer robotics companies; eight had a public privacy policy that applied to their products. Two authors read each policy four times and independently gave a score from 0 to 3 for each of the eight privacy principles of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), then reached a consensus. The documents were accessed between April 2019 and April 2020."
findings:
  - "Out of a maximum of 24 points, scores ranged from 6 to 14. Only two companies reached a score of 3 on any principle."
  - "The collection limitation principle had the worst scores: six of the eight policies received zero. Take-it-or-leave-it consent prevails, with no choice by feature."
  - "All the policies state what data they collect and why, but several use vague terms and open-ended retention periods. None identifies the third parties with whom data is shared."
  - "Only half of the policies cite rules, standards or ethical guidelines. The country of the head office did not seem to affect the score, and the length of the text does not determine how well the principles are covered."
  - "The authors recommend stating what the robot processes locally and what it sends to the company, using layered and just-in-time notices, and separating the rights of the buyer and the user when they are different people."
limitations: "The sample has only eight companies, and the scoring is qualitative and subjective, as the authors acknowledge. The study analyzes what the policies say, not what the companies do with the data. It does not cover industrial robots or workers' data."
