whyItMatters: "The text works as a map of applications for robots that operate close to people outside the factory: hospitals, laboratories and the pharmaceutical industry. It also gathers the issues that arise when a robot gets close to patients and staff: privacy, cybersecurity, liability for harm and cost."
method: "The authors searched for studies from 2004 to 2023 in four databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus and Google Scholar), in English, on robotics in surgery, telemedicine and rehabilitation. A table summarizes 47 included studies. The synthesis is narrative and organized into five themes: surgery, telemedicine, rehabilitation, ethics and barriers."
findings:
  - "The review reports that healthcare professionals perform monotonous, low-value tasks that could be handed over to cobots. In the pharmaceutical industry, cobots already work in quality control, assembly and packaging; in laboratories, they handle materials in sterile environments."
  - "For infectious diseases, the review points to three gains: less contact with infected patients, more clinical capacity and fewer entries into the isolation area, which reduces the use of protective equipment."
  - "Robotic surgery is associated with fewer incisions, less blood loss and shorter hospital stays, but the outcome depends on the type of surgery and the surgeon's skill. Purchase and operating costs and lengthy training are barriers."
  - "Most assistive robots are still prototypes. In hospitals, the uses described include telepresence, measuring vital signs, transporting supplies and medication, cleaning rooms and rehabilitation with repetitive movements."
  - "Open issues include the privacy of patient data, the risk that a hacked robot causes physical harm, and liability shared among manufacturer, programmer and user. The review argues that the robot should complement the professional, not replace them."
limitations: "This is a narrative review, with no quality assessment of the studies and no data of its own. It deals almost entirely with service, surgical and rehabilitation robots, and uses the term cobot loosely. The physical safety of contact between person and robot and the working conditions of healthcare staff are not analyzed."
