whyItMatters: "The review brings together what is known about how workers react to robots and exoskeletons: cognitive load, trust, stress, fatigue. People who work with industrial cobots will find results here on autonomy, failures and communication, but should keep in mind that the study is about construction and that robot arms are a minority in it."
method: "The authors carried out a systematic review following the PRISMA protocol in two databases (Scopus and Web of Science), covering 2000 to January 2026. From 1682 unique records, they read 411 in full and included 163 studies, 14 of them found through citation tracking. One researcher extracted and coded the results by theme and a second reviewed the work."
findings:
  - "The review found 21 human factors in four categories: cognitive, affective, physical, and acceptance and usability. Cognitive factors, such as cognitive load, attention and trust, are the most studied. The output is recent: 12 publications in 2021 and 53 in 2025."
  - "In tasks shared with a robot arm, more automation was associated with more fatigue, stress, effort and cognitive load than manual execution. The semi-autonomous mode, with the person in the loop, strengthened trust."
  - "Robot failures reduce trust and increase cognitive load and negative emotions. Overtrust, in turn, was associated with more unsafe behavior."
  - "Exoskeletons relieve load at one point of the body but bring trade-offs: discomfort at the chest and attachment points, restricted movement and, in active lumbar support models, more cognitive load."
  - "As for validity, 45% of the studies took place in a virtual environment, 40% in a lab and only 9% on a real jobsite; 95% lasted less than an 8-hour shift. Three quarters of the studies that report experience used students and novices."
limitations: "The review is about construction: robot arms appear in only 26 studies, and most of the work covers ground robots, drones and exoskeletons, so the link to industrial cobots is by analogy. The authors leave out cost, workforce readiness and technical requirements. The studies reviewed monitor physiological data without discussing privacy or data ownership."
