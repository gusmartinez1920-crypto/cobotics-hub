whyItMatters: "A cobot leaves the factory with safety measures, but that condition changes once it gets a gripper and is placed in a workstation. The paper brings together in one place what the integrator needs to consider, and notes that ergonomics and efficiency are often left out of the design."
method: "The authors compiled and classified guidelines from international standards, research papers and use cases, following the logic of concurrent engineering, which treats product, assembly cycle, robot, layout and operator at the same time. The safety guidelines come from standards such as ISO 12100, ISO 10218-2 and ISO/TS 15066; those on physical ergonomics from ISO/TR 12295; those on cognitive ergonomics from eight studies; those on efficiency from five sources on design for assembly and machine vision."
findings:
  - "The layout should meet three general requirements: minimize occupational risks, especially mechanical ones; maximize the operator's physical and cognitive well-being; and minimize the time and cost of manual, robotic and collaborative tasks."
  - "The safety guidelines deal only with mechanical hazards, in three groups: entrapment of body parts, collision, and falling parts of the system. They include different speed limits for each type of contact, force limiting, rounded corners, padding and signaling of robot motion."
  - "In physical ergonomics, the guidelines cover three problems: overload of the arms in repetitive tasks, overload in manual lifting, and static or awkward postures."
  - "In cognitive ergonomics, they recommend smooth trajectories similar to those of the human arm, avoiding high speeds, using the smallest robot possible, informing the operator about the robot's speed and involving the operator in defining the layout and activities."
  - "The authors note that mechanical hazards are covered by several international technical documents, while cognitive aspects are still at an early research stage."
limitations: "The paper does not describe how the sources were searched and selected, and the guidelines were not tested at a real workstation. The checklist they are meant to produce is left for future work. The tables do not give numerical values for the limits or address conflicts between guidelines, such as limiting speed and reducing cycle time."
