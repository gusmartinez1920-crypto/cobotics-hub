whyItMatters: "A construction site is very different from a factory: the environment changes all the time, tasks vary and many people move around. The review shows that rules designed for industrial robots are not enough there and gathers what research has tried so far. It offers a contrast for those who know factory cobots and want to understand another sector."
method: "The authors carried out a mixed-method review. They searched the Scopus database for studies from 2005 to 2025 and, from 14,128 initial records, reached 287 after filters, abstract screening and full-text reading. They analyzed the set with bibliometric software (countries, most cited sources, keywords and their evolution) and produced a qualitative synthesis in five themes."
findings:
  - "Output was low and irregular from 2005 to 2018 and has grown steadily since 2019. The United States leads with 91 papers, followed by China with 41."
  - "The oldest and largest research cluster is remote control of machinery. The most recent ones deal with collaborative robots and robot speed, digital twins, and risk assessment."
  - "The synthesis arrived at five themes: remotely operated intelligent machinery; hazard analysis and risk assessment; digital twins for monitoring; cognitive and psychological effects; and organizational management."
  - "According to the review, the ISO 10218 standard focuses on mechanical parameters of industrial robots, such as contact force and speed, and is insufficient for the construction site, which is dynamic and involves many actors."
  - "Reviewed studies indicate that fast or unpredictable robot movements cause stress and reduce awareness of the surroundings. One cited work, which plans the robot's path by predicting where workers will move, reduced collisions by 23%."
limitations: "The search used a single database, English only and empirical studies only, as the authors acknowledge. The review shows that remote control and digital twins remained at the prototype, simulator or laboratory stage, with little validation on real sites, and that studies on psychological effects are fragmented. The concept of collaboration used is broad and includes teleoperated machines and exoskeletons; the collaborative arm is a minority."
