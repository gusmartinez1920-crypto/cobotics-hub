whyItMatters: "The lack of prepared people is cited as a barrier to robot adoption, and research tends to look more at technology than at training. This study offers a ranked list of knowledge, skills and abilities that can guide courses and training programs. The sector is construction, and the experts deal mostly with drones, exoskeletons and autonomous equipment; carrying the results over to factory cobots is up to the reader."
method: "The authors drew 42 competencies from the literature: 20 knowledge areas, 10 skills and 12 abilities. They then applied the Delphi method, a consultation of experts in rounds, with professionals in the United States selected by education, experience and contact with robots: 18 qualified, 16 in the first round and 13 in the second. Each competency received an importance rating, and the final ranking combined these ratings with the participants' choices."
findings:
  - "The panel reached consensus in all three categories, with good consistency of responses (Cronbach's alpha of 0.89 for knowledge, 0.76 for skills and 0.82 for abilities)."
  - "Among knowledge areas, the most valued were the human-robot interface, safety and standards for collaboration, robot applications in construction, task planning and the robot control system. Ethics and regulation ranked among the last."
  - "Among skills, safety management and task planning led, followed by technical skill. Programming, data analysis and applying machine learning came last."
  - "Among abilities, safety awareness was the most valued, followed by continuous learning, problem solving, critical thinking and spatial awareness. Cultural and social awareness ranked last."
  - "The authors interpret that the sector prioritizes immediate operational concerns and that programming carries little weight because most workers do not program robots. They recommend courses on robot operation, virtual simulation, micro-credentials and safety training in companies."
limitations: "The panel is small, lost participants between rounds and brings together managers, engineers and researchers, not the workers who interact with the robots. The authors themselves say the result indicates expert consensus, not that of the sector. The study does not measure the effect of these competencies on performance."
