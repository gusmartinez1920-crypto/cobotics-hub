whyItMatters: "The review works as a map of the field: it separates avoiding contact, detecting and mitigating contact, physical ergonomics, and cognitive and organizational ergonomics. For anyone designing a cell, it shows that making the robot safe is not enough if the person does not accept or trust the system."
method: "The authors carried out a systematic literature review in the Scopus database, with journal papers in English published from 2015 to 2018 in engineering and computer science. They started from 7,589 documents on collaborative robotics and, after filters and screening by three independent researchers, arrived at 55 papers. They classified each paper by theme and measured the output and annual growth of each group."
findings:
  - "Research falls into four groups: avoiding dangerous contact (40.3% of the classifications), detecting and mitigating contact (23.9%), cognitive and organizational ergonomics (19.4%) and physical ergonomics (16.4%)."
  - "In safety, research favors prevention, before contact, over protection, which reduces the effect of contact. The most frequent theme is motion planning and control."
  - "Ergonomics is growing faster: in 2018 there were 16 safety papers against 13 on ergonomics. Physical ergonomics went from no papers in 2015 to 8 in 2018."
  - "In physical ergonomics, the main theme is allocating and sequencing tasks between person and robot using ergonomic criteria. In cognitive and organizational ergonomics, it is metrics and tests of worker acceptance and trust."
  - "The authors warn that faster and safer robots may lead operators to work closer to them, which can harm the psychological side of collaboration and efficiency itself. They therefore propose aligning the two lines of research."
limitations: "The review uses a single database, only journal papers in engineering and computer science, and covers only 2015 to 2018, so ergonomics published in other fields was left out. The importance assigned to each theme measures only how often it appears in the papers, not how much it matters to the worker."
