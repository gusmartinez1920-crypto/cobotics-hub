whyItMatters: "A cobot can meet every force and speed limit and still leave the operator tense. This survey gathers what experiments indicate about distance, speed, predictability of motion and operator control, and it works as a checklist for designing a cell. It also shows how to measure this perception, from questionnaires to physiological signals."
method: "The authors reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings and book chapters in English published up to 2020 that reported experiments with people sharing space with a robot, physical or in virtual reality, and some measure of perceived safety. They started from the studies cited in five earlier surveys, followed citations backward and forward, and completed the set with direct searches in four databases, reaching 114 papers. They covered six types of system: industrial manipulators, mobile robots, mobile manipulators, humanoids, drones and autonomous vehicles."
findings:
  - "The survey found many names for the same topic: psychological, mental or subjective safety, trust and comfort, and, on the negative side, stress, fear, anxiety and surprise. Comfort is the most widespread term across all robot types."
  - "The measures fall into four categories: questionnaires, physiological signals (such as heart rate and skin electrical response), observation of behavior, and devices on which the person indicates in the moment what they feel. Many studies used questionnaires alone."
  - "Industrial manipulators are the type with the most studies. For them, perceived safety improves with greater distance, lower speed, a smaller robot, fluent and predictable motion, advance notice of motion, the ability to decide when the motion starts and previous experience with the task."
  - "One of the studies reviewed concluded that, to avoid mental strain, the person should stay at least 2 m from the robot and the robot should not exceed 0.5 m/s; announcing the motion in advance reduced mental strain. Another observed more stress when participants were not in control."
  - "The authors list eight factors common to all robot types: distance, speed, speed proportional to distance, direction of approach, size and appearance, fluency and predictability of motion, communication and smooth contacts."
limitations: "Most of the experiments reviewed were short and carried out in the laboratory; only three studies lasted up to two months, so adaptation over time has hardly been studied. Industrial robots are only one of the six categories, and the survey does not follow a protocol with counts at each stage or assess the quality of the studies."
