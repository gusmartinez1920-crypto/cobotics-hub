whyItMatters: "The more closely a person works with the robot, the more the interface matters: it stops being only a programming tool and becomes a way to exchange information and confirm actions. The study helps in choosing among screen, gesture and voice, and shows where industrial research is still behind."
method: "The authors carried out a narrative literature review, organized by type of interface (gestures, voice, graphical interfaces and combinations) and split into industrial robots and service robots. The text draws on 53 references, mentions ongoing research projects and ends with the authors' opinion, based on their own work with stationary and mobile industrial robots."
findings:
  - "The importance of the interface grows with the degree of collaboration and with the robot's autonomy. In close collaboration, it has to serve for exchanging information and confirming actions, not only for programming."
  - "In industry, commercial interfaces are mainly the touchscreens supplied with the robot, now more intuitive thanks to apps and programming blocks. The authors report that manufacturers do not yet offer gestures or speech recognition."
  - "In the studies reviewed, gestures are used mainly to move the robot to a position or change its direction, and voice to give commands. Voice is usually combined with gestures or gaze to improve recognition."
  - "There is more research on interfaces for service robots than for industrial robots. In industry the task is complex, gesture or speech alone is often not enough, and application scenarios vary widely, which makes solutions hard to reuse."
  - "The authors propose first defining what information needs to be exchanged and only then choosing the mode of interaction. They point to open issues: little user involvement in interface design, the lack of evidence of the benefit that interaction brings, and the privacy of speech and gesture data."
limitations: "The review does not say how the studies were searched for and selected, and part of the conclusions is the authors' stated opinion. About half of the text deals with service robots, and the study does not relate the interfaces to collaborative operation modes or to safety standards."
