whyItMatters: "Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) are the main market for cobots, but there are almost no studies of the adoption decision at company level. This one indicates where manufacturers, integrators and trainers can act: reduce perceived difficulty, let the company try the robot and see it working, and provide technical support. Contrary to expectations, competitive pressure and government support did not matter."
method: "The authors administered an online questionnaire to managers of SMEs in Guangdong province from March to May 2022 and obtained 242 valid responses out of 373 distributed. The questionnaire measured ten factors related to the technology, the organization and the company's environment, as well as the intention to adopt cobots. The relationships were tested with a structural equation model."
findings:
  - "Together, the ten factors explain about 52% of the variance in the intention to adopt cobots (R² = 0.516)."
  - "Perceived complexity is the factor with the greatest weight and the only negative one (β = −0.326). The authors interpret it as SMEs' concern about the knowledge and skills required, such as programming."
  - "Positive effects come from perceived advantage (β = 0.231), the ability to try the cobot out (β = 0.227), the ability to see it working (β = 0.192) and compatibility with the company (β = 0.184)."
  - "Top management support (β = 0.150), availability of financial and human resources (β = 0.149) and supplier support (β = 0.181) also favor the intention to adopt."
  - "Competitive pressure and government support had no significant effect. The authors attribute this to the early stage of adoption among Chinese SMEs and to innovation policies that favor large companies."
limitations: "The study measures the intention to adopt, not adoption or actual use. Only managers responded, all from a single province, and the paper does not report the companies' sector or size. The authors themselves acknowledge that the model leaves out factors such as the ethics and safety of human-robot collaboration; cost and return were not measured either."
