whyItMatters: "The study shows an unusual use of a cobot: as a teaching resource for children who cannot yet read or write. It also offers lessons that apply to any voice interface, at school or in a factory: a fixed waiting time and differences in pronunciation get in the way of people who speak differently from what the system expects."
method: "A pre-test and post-test study with no control group, with 34 children aged 4 to 6 from a small town in southern Chile; 33 completed all sessions. The robot was a Ufactory Lite 6 arm with a suction cup, microphone and speaker, which interpreted commands with a language model and located objects by computer vision. There were five one-hour sessions. The authors applied a 15-question computational thinking test and analyzed 18 videos of an assembly challenge."
findings:
  - "The mean test score rose from 8.76 to 10.12 points, out of 15, a statistically significant difference, after one week of activities."
  - "In the challenge of assembling a toy with the robot, 30 of 33 children broke the problem into parts, 28 generalized what they had learned, 26 arranged the steps in sequence and 15 corrected errors in their own commands."
  - "Almost all of them collaborated with one another: 30 of 33 took turns speaking and helped each other, and 32 of 33 persisted with the task."
  - "The voice interface partly failed: children aged 4 and 5 needed more time than the 6 seconds available to say the command, and pronunciation difficulties forced them to repeat it."
  - "For safety, the operating area was marked off, an adult stood between the child and the robot, and the speed was reduced."
limitations: "The authors themselves treat the results as preliminary: the sample is small and there is no control group, so the effect of the program cannot be separated from other factors. The test measures computational thinking in general, not the ability to work with a cobot."
