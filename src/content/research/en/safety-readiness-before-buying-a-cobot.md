whyItMatters: "The mandatory risk assessment only happens in the design phase, when the purchase has already been decided. This study proposes looking earlier and more broadly: people, organization and environment, in addition to the machine. For a company considering its first cobot, it offers a guide to what it needs to know and where its gaps are."
method: "The authors combine a narrative literature review with their earlier research on risk factors and, from there, do the conceptual modeling of a self-assessment tool based on a maturity grid. Only the first two development phases are covered: preparing and building the model. Evaluation with experts and tests in companies are left for later."
findings:
  - "The authors define safety readiness as how prepared an organization is to deploy a cobot, in terms of awareness and knowledge of the safety of the whole system."
  - "There is an apparent contradiction: looking only at machine safety creates a false sense of safety; at the same time, the cobot seems too safe when force limits and stops interrupt production."
  - "The proposed tool uses five classes of risk factors as its dimensions: technology, human, collaborative workspace, enterprise and external, with 13 subclasses."
  - "The grid has four levels, from having no idea of the risk factors to having new tools or tools adapted to cobots. Two additional questions check whether the company has the knowledge or knows where to find it."
  - "Trust in automation is treated as the main driver of psychosocial well-being. The authors recommend communication, training, simulation and experimentation before deployment."
limitations: "The tool has not yet been validated: the descriptors for each level are illustrated for a single subdimension, and evaluation by experts and companies is still to be done. It is static and restricted to the phase before purchase, so it does not capture changes after installation. The review behind it does not report a search protocol or the number of studies."
