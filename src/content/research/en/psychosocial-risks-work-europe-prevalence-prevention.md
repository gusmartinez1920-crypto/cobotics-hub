method: "This is a synthesis report by two European agencies. It combines a survey of workers interviewed at home in 2010, a 2009 telephone survey of about 36,000 health and safety managers in establishments with 10 or more employees, policy examples from six countries and a narrative review of workplace interventions. Associations between working conditions and health were estimated with logistic regression."
findings:
  - "25% of European workers report work-related stress for all or most of their working time, and a similar share say that work harms their health."
  - "The most common risks come from the type of task and from work intensity. Among plant and machine operators, 62% report monotonous tasks and 57% repetitive tasks. Those who report work intensity above the median are about three times more likely to report stress."
  - "48% of workers went through process or technology changes in the previous three years, and 13% say they need more training to cope with their tasks. Needing training is associated with more stress and dissatisfaction."
  - "Almost 80% of managers are concerned about stress, but only 25% to 30% of establishments have procedures for psychosocial risks. Manufacturing and construction are the sectors with the fewest measures."
  - "Where employees are directly involved, 91% of managers consider the actions effective, against 59% where they are not. The report points to combining changes at the source of the risk with support for people's coping capacity as the most effective approach."
limitations: "The data are from 2009 and 2010 and come from the respondents' own answers, collected at a single point in time, which, according to the report, tends to underestimate the effect of work on health. The two surveys use different methods and samples, and most intervention programs have not been evaluated systematically."
