whyItMatters: "Anyone considering a cobot hears messages that do not fit together: the robot is inherently safe, it is too limited because it is safe, and it brings new risks. The study names this tension and helps explain why the decision is hard. It also suggests bringing safety knowledge in before the purchase."
method: "The authors reviewed the literature in two databases (Web of Science and Scopus), in English, from 2011 to June 2021. After screening, they analyzed 35 texts in full, 10 of which deal specifically with adoption factors for industrial cobots. They classified the factors using an existing framework (internal, external and technological) and added data from an exploratory master's survey with 74 respondents and interviews with two manufacturers."
findings:
  - "Research on cobot adoption is recent and shallow: the 35 texts analyzed start only in 2016, and the empirical studies are qualitative or exploratory, with few observations."
  - "Six of the ten texts on adoption factors treat safety as a barrier, but none discusses its role in detail. Lack of knowledge appears as the main barrier in more than one study."
  - "The authors describe cobot safety as a property of the whole system, which depends on technological, human, workspace, enterprise and external factors. That complexity is a barrier in itself."
  - "They identify three conflicting views: for machine safety, the cobot is inherently safe; for operations management, it is too safe, because the limits reduce speed, payload and productivity; for the human-centered view, it is less safe, because of the new risks."
  - "The authors propose an accessible tool to assess a company's safety readiness as early as the stage when it is considering the purchase, inspired by maturity models."
limitations: "The readiness tool is only a proposal, with no dimensions, levels or validation. The conflict between safety and productivity is described but not resolved, and the idea that the cobot is too safe rests on few sources. The review did not assess the quality of the studies and offers no data of its own on cost or return."
