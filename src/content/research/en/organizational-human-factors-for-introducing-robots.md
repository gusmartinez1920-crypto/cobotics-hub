whyItMatters: "Many automation projects fail because of people and organization, not the machine. The study provides a list of what to look after before and during deployment: communication, operator participation, training, management support and the relationship with the union. It is useful for anyone planning to install a cobot, although the case studied involves a welding robot in a cell."
method: "The work had two stages. First, a review of the literature on implementing advanced manufacturing technology and on organizational change, which are related fields, to build the framework of factors. Then, a case study at a large aerospace component manufacturer in the United Kingdom that was replacing manual welding of a component with a robot. Twelve people were interviewed, including operators, engineers, system designers, management and the union, and the answers were analyzed with the seven factors as a guide."
findings:
  - "The framework brings together seven factors: communication of the change, operator participation, training, a champion who promotes the project, worker empowerment, senior management support and union involvement."
  - "In the case, almost everyone said that operator participation eased the transition and that it would have been better to involve operators from the concept phase. It created a sense of ownership and less resistance."
  - "Communication explained well why the process was being automated but did not cover the impact on the daily routine. Training some key users at the integrator's site built confidence, and they passed the knowledge on to their colleagues."
  - "The barriers were late involvement of the union, taking operators out of production for development work, and an integrator that did not understand the complexity of the manual process, which led to extra cost for a vision system."
  - "The case revealed a factor missing from the literature: capturing the variability of the manual process before the change. The welders had skills for dealing with variations in geometry and thickness, and that knowledge needed to reach the integrator."
limitations: "This is a single case study, and the authors themselves acknowledge that it does not allow generalization. The case is not human-robot collaboration in the strict sense: it concerns replacing manual welding with a robot in a cell, and the factors are carried over by analogy. Operators' workload and stress were not measured."
