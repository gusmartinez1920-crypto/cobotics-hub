whyItMatters: "The review shows where research on cobot safety is concentrated and where there is little material. For anyone deploying a cell, the message is practical: many published solutions have not yet been tested in factories, and the risk assessment used day to day falls short of what research proposes."
method: "The authors carried out a bibliometric analysis followed by a literature review, started in November 2022. In the Scopus database (1980 to 2022) they arrived at 1,847 papers on collaborative robots from highly ranked journals, analyzed by country, journal and keywords. With safety and health filters, plus a complementary search in Web of Science, 58 papers remained and were read in full. There was no quality assessment of the studies and no gray literature was included."
findings:
  - "The 58 papers fall into five areas: collisions (35), ergonomics (8), cognitive workload and mental stress (7), hazard analysis and risk assessment (6) and cyberattacks (2)."
  - "Manufacturing is the most studied application area, and assembly is the most frequent task, followed by quality inspection and material handling."
  - "In risk assessment, the review found a wide gap between research and practice: companies use simple approaches, often commercial software that rarely covers everything the standards require."
  - "One of the studies reviewed analyzed 369 accidents in Korea in which operators were injured by robots. The most frequent direct cause was unsafe worker behavior, but cramped workstations, wrong layout, tight deadlines and missing procedures also contributed."
  - "Studies reviewed link stress to the cobot approaching quickly and unpredictably, and report a higher heart rate as the distance decreases. Physical barriers increase perceived safety."
limitations: "The review does not assess the quality of the included studies and leaves out gray literature. Most of the solutions described were validated only in the lab, and the authors call for studies in real settings. Psychosocial risks get little attention: the human factor appears mainly as a source of risk."
